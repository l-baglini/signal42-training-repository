The craft that stuck

Six sessions, six habits.

A1

Tier by risk

"Airplane code" you never let fail, vs. make-life-better code you run fast on. Rigour follows the tier.

A2

Context is the game

Feed the model the architecture, constraints and dependencies — and curate the window as it fills.

A3

Direct many, not one

Autocomplete, chat or agent — and giving the agent hands through Git and MCP.

A4

The spec directs

Intent, acceptance, non-goals, done. A team with no spec builds the wrong thing, fast.

A5

You own the check

Read the diff, set the depth by risk, let a second model review the first. Solved isn't shipped.

A6

Make it travel

Specs, checks and a SKILL.md that teaches any model your codebase — the base the building track stands on.

Track A was about speed — using AI well. Track B is about product — putting AI inside what you ship. Six sessions, three moves.

Build it · Sessions B1–B2

First, build the agents.

B1

Agentic workflows & MCP

What an agent actually is, where it breaks, and wiring your own tools and data in over MCP. You'll build a small one that does a real job.

For Onetag: an agent that plugs into your own systems and data — the first step to AI that works inside the product.

B2

Orchestration & harnesses

What frameworks still do for you, what the model now handles on its own, and why architecture and dependencies beat memorising packages.

For Onetag: the backbone for multi-step features — chaining steps reliably, not resting on one clever prompt.

Trust it · Sessions B3–B4

Then put the safeguards around them.

B3

Eval, testing & prompt validation

Tests you can trust instead of eyeballing output — measuring whether an AI feature is actually good enough, tied straight back to your A1 risk tiers.

For Onetag: anything near bidding or yield can't be eyeballed — evals are how it earns its place in the stack.

B4

Observability

Watching an AI feature once it's live — what to monitor, what "drifting" looks like, and catching it before your users do.

For Onetag: a live classifier or a model in the loop needs eyes on it in production, wired to your evals.

Feed it knowledge · Sessions B5–B6

Then give it your knowledge.

B5

RAG fundamentals

Chunking, embeddings and retrieval — plus the honest question most people skip: when RAG is the right tool, and when it's overkill.

For Onetag: retrieval over your own docs and taxonomy — grounding answers in what you actually know.

B6

Vector databases + capstone

How vector stores work, the trade-offs that matter, and a short capstone that pulls B1–B5 into one build.

For Onetag: the store behind retrieval at scale — and a build you can point straight at real work.

The Summer Build

Your idea. Any area. Your own time.

  • Any area you like — your own itch, a passion project, whatever pulls you. Your call.
  • Any language, any stack, any size — a weekend hack or something you nurse all summer.
  • Your own time, your own access. Completely optional — nothing here is expected of you.
How you build it now

Your repos carry the craft now.

  • A spec that directed it — the intent the agent built to.
  • A review pass on the AI's work — you owned the check.
  • A CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md — so a fresh model could pick it up tomorrow.

You're recognised for all of it — the skills you've built, not just the finished thing. Make the thinking travel with the code.
